# Follow-up results: environment-lock ranking + climate-analog experiment

Both run on the text/structure spine only. No imagery.

## Analysis 2 (lead with this — it's the strongest) — Climate-analog natural experiment

**Design.** Take pairs of traditions that are **>5,000 km apart** (no plausible recent
cultural contact). Split them into:
- **climate-analog pairs**: same Köppen class AND within 5° of absolute latitude
  (e.g. two separate boreal zones on different continents), and
- **distance-matched control pairs**: different climate, resampled to have the *same
  geographic-distance distribution* as the analogs.

Because distance is matched, the only systematic difference between the two groups is
**climate similarity**. Then measure how many motifs each pair shares (Jaccard),
separately for pareidolic vs non-pareidolic motifs.

**Result (`fig7_climate_analog.png`):**

| motif set | analog pairs | control pairs | lift | p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **pareidolic** | 0.0566 | 0.0506 | **+11.9%** | **0.0002** |
| non-pareidolic | 0.0246 | 0.0250 | −1.3% | 0.81 |

**This is a clean dissociation, and it's the best evidence yet.** Two cultures on
opposite sides of the planet, with no possible contact, share *pareidolic* mythemes
~12% more often when they live in the same climate than when they don't — highly
significant. For *ordinary* mythology there is no such effect (−1%, p=0.81): once you
remove the possibility of contact, climate does nothing for non-pareidolic motifs.

That is exactly what the hypothesis predicts: figure-in-nature mythology is partly
generated by the environment itself (convergent invention in similar landscapes/skies),
while ordinary mythology only spreads by contact. When you switch contact off (far-apart
pairs), the environmental signal survives *only* for pareidolic motifs.

This is a near-experimental result from observational data, and it's the kind of finding
that motivates and pre-validates the entire imagery program.

## Analysis 1 — Environment-lock ranking of every motif

**Design.** For each motif, score:
- environmental signal = mutual information between motif presence and biome,
- diffusion signal = spatial autocorrelation (Moran's I, 1000 km Gaussian kernel),
then `env_lock = z(env) − z(diffusion)`, **z-scored within prevalence deciles** so common
and rare motifs are compared fairly. High env_lock = environment-locked (pareidolic-
candidate); low = contact/diffusion-driven.

**Validation result (`fig6_environment_lock.png`):** the keyword-flagged pareidolic
motifs score significantly higher on env_lock than the rest
(mean +0.096 vs −0.016, Δ=+0.112, permutation p=0.029). So the independent,
distribution-based measure agrees with the description-based keyword flag — two different
methods converge on the same set of motifs. That cross-validation is reassuring.

**But read the top of the list critically.** The top-15 most environment-locked motifs
mix obvious pareidolic celestial motifs ("Altair is the Sun's messenger", "Water-carrier
in the Moon", "Sky hunters pursue an ungulate", "Ursus major is an ungulate") with things
that are environment-locked for a *different* reason — e.g. "Trickster-fox/jackal/coyote"
(the animal cast simply reflects which predators live there) and "Cutting tree to make a
canoe" (material culture, not pareidolia). This is an important distinction:

- **environment-locked because of pareidolia** (the sky/landscape *looks like* the figure)
- **environment-locked because of available materials/fauna** (you tell stories about the
  animals and resources that are actually present)

Both track environment, so env_lock alone can't separate them — and this is a real
confound for the whole project that we flagged earlier, now showing up concretely in the
data. The celestial subset ("figure in the moon/stars/sun") is the cleanest pareidolic
class because the sky is ~the same everywhere, so any *regional* variation in sky-figure
motifs is harder to explain by "different animals live here."

**Actionable output:** `motif_environment_lock.csv` ranks all ~1,900 sufficiently-common
motifs. Use it to build a curated pareidolic list: start from the high-env_lock motifs,
then manually keep the ones that are genuinely about *perceiving a figure in a natural
feature* and drop the fauna/material-culture ones. That curated list replaces the crude
keyword flag and should sharpen every downstream result, including re-running Analysis 2.

## What this pair of results means together

- The partial-Mantel result (fig5) said: pareidolic mythology leans more on environment,
  relative to diffusion, than ordinary mythology.
- The climate-analog experiment (fig7) sharpened it to a near-experimental claim: with
  contact ruled out, *only* pareidolic mythology still tracks climate.
- The env-lock ranking (fig6) cross-validated the pareidolic flag but also exposed the
  fauna/materials confound we must control for.

All three point the same direction and were obtained before any image was touched. The
imagery stage now has (a) motivation, (b) a curated target list of the cleanest pareidolic
motifs, and (c) a specific confound to design against (separate "looks-like" pareidolia
from "lives-here" fauna).

## Caveats
- Köppen class + 5° latitude is a coarse climate-analog definition; tighten with WorldClim
  bioclim vectors per coordinate.
- Jaccard shared-motif fractions are small in absolute terms (motifs are sparse); the
  *contrast* between analog and control, and between motif classes, is the finding.
- The fauna/materials confound (above) is not yet removed from the pareidolic set; doing so
  will likely strengthen the dissociation, not weaken it, but must be done before publishing.
- Documentation/ethnographic-effort bias still applies to absolute counts.
