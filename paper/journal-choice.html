The ten

ranked by impact × minimal change
01

Royal Society Open Science

The Royal Society

Impact · High ≈ £1,400 — not free Rework · Light

The precedent is almost uncomfortably exact: Shibasaki, Nakadai & Nakawake (2024, RSOS 11(5):231577) used Berezkin’s catalogue, geocoded, against 93M GBIF records, concluding ecology constrains folklore. Same corpus, same architecture, adjacent thesis — already through review here. No word limit, so your 9,359 words and 10 figures go as they stand. Soundness-only review would interrogate your control stack rather than gatekeep on register.

Costs you: a retitle and a presubmission enquiry citing 231577 by name. The catch: RSOS is born-gold and explicitly excluded from Subscribe-to-Open — the one Royal Society venue that fits best is the one that isn’t free.

02

Evolutionary Human Sciences

Cambridge University Press

Impact · High Free — verified Rework · Moderate

The only venue that is simultaneously open to unsolicited empirical work, staffed with reviewers who can evaluate a matched-permutation null, and verifiably free: CRKN–Cambridge covers “all Cambridge hybrid and gold journals” — decisive, since EHS is fully gold (list APC USD 3,450).

Costs you: trim 9,359 → 8,000 words (−15%), 10 → 8 display items, abstract 323 → 200, APA restyle. References are fine at 40 < 75. Reframe the breadth gradient in cultural-evolutionary terms (it already is a differential-retention result) and cite Bromham & Yaxley (2023) — the Galton’s-problem standard your Glottolog swap-null already meets.

03

Cognitive Science

Wiley · Cognitive Science Society

Impact · Med–High Free — hybrid Rework · Moderate

The desk is unusually sympathetic, and that is worth real money: the Gibson / 4E / cognitive-ecology framing lands with a friendly reader rather than a puzzled one. The “no human subjects” worry is refuted by the journal’s own recent output — participant-free corpus analyses of symbolic material are a live genre here.

Costs you: a front-matter reframe toward an individual-level question (“what does transmission strip out, and what makes a motif portable?”), submitted as an Extended Article. Verify first: the Society, not Wiley, holds copyright — email licensing@crkn.ca before relying on the waiver.

04

Journal of the Royal Society Interface

The Royal Society

Impact · High Free via S2O 2026 Rework · Heavy

Your own citation lives here — Constant et al. (2018, RSIF 15(141):20170685) — inside a Friston-adjacent cluster, so the niche-construction framing is native rather than imported. Free for any author in 2026, but by Subscribe-to-Open, not by any agreement your universities hold.

Costs you: an 8,000-word cap that includes references and acknowledgements, counted mechanically by staff before an editor reads a sentence. Every RSIF culture paper also carries a physical-science credential — a generative model, or scale as the contribution. Promoting the breadth gradient to a dose-response ordering is the cheapest way to qualify.

05

Cross-Cultural Research

SAGE · HRAF-sponsored — not on your sheet

Impact · Low–Med Free — SAGE hybrid Rework · None

The venue that takes this artifact as written, today, with no new analysis. Its scope explicitly names “human ecology,” and its only constitutive demand is that measured differences among cultures be linked to other measured differences among cultures — which is exactly what biome and myth–image alignment are.

Costs you: nothing but ceiling. This is the floor of the list, and the right answer only if the reframing budget runs out. Worth your own 20 minutes of verification before ranking it.

06

Proceedings of the Royal Society B

The Royal Society

Impact · Very High Free via S2O 2026 Rework · Heavy

Ross, Greenhill & Atkinson’s folktale population-structure paper sits here (280:1756), so subject matter isn’t the constraint — inference machinery is. Proc B takes human-culture papers when the inference is biological in kind: AMOVA, Bayesian ancestral-state reconstruction.

Costs you: genuinely new analysis — inverting the biome-swap null into a phylogenetic comparative model (Pagel’s λ, ancestral-state) on Glottolog. Telling detail: the trickster paper carried 93M occurrence records and still went to RSOS, not here. Don’t spend the shot on this draft.

07

Journal of Cross-Cultural Psychology

SAGE

Impact · Med Free — hybrid Rework · Heavy

The question is native; the artifact is not. Berry’s ecocultural framework runs ecology → cultural adaptation → individual behaviour. You deliver links one and two and stop precisely where JCCP begins.

Costs you: a psychological dependent variable you don’t have — every non-participant precedent keeps a construct at one pole (Hofstede individualism, WVS values). Your poles are biome and embedding geometry; neither is psychological. Downgrade your “High confidence” here. Corroborating: the flagship Berezkin paper (Michalopoulos & Xue) went to economics.

08

Behavioral and Brain Sciences

Cambridge University Press

Impact · Very High $0 but green-only — no gold OA Rework · A different paper

The leverage is real and personal: Veissière, Constant, Ramstead, Friston & Kirmayer (BBS 43, 2020) is your introduction’s backbone, and three of those authors are McGill — your co-author’s own community. Costs you: the wrong order. BBS takes empirical reports “rarely,” and rewards systematizing a body of research — you are the first result, the exact profile it routes elsewhere. Do it after publication, via a 1,000–2,000-word Target Article Proposal. Note it offers no gold or hybrid OA at all.

Topics in Cognitive Science

Wiley

No door Impact · High

10

Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B

The Royal Society

No door Impact · Very High

Aspiration, not a step “Does not consider unsolicited submissions.” Theme issues only, all commissioned. This is the best intellectual home on your entire list — Claidière, Scott-Phillips & Sperber’s canonical cultural-attraction statement, which your introduction is now built on, is literally here — and there is no door.

Reality: publication here is a downstream consequence of becoming visible in the cultural-attraction community, not a move available with this manuscript. Guest-editing a theme issue is a 1–3 year, normally senior-led project with <50% acceptance.

Ruled out, with cause

high on the sheet, wrong in practice

Cognition

Gate is “bearing on the functioning of the mind.” Its corpus precedents model cognition — anchored in human free-association norms. SigLIP-2 is your instrument; nobody claims its geometry resembles human representation. Zero folklore and zero vision-language precedent in its entire record.

BioScience

“Does not publish primary research articles.” Your paper is nothing but new data. Plus a mandatory $1,500 publication fee — not an APC, so no read-and-publish agreement covers it. Right publisher, wrong title.

Biology Letters

Strictly dominated. 3,500 words with figure legends counted, against a paper whose whole credibility is its control architecture. Strip the controls, get rejected for lacking them. Proc B has identical S2O coverage and no such cap.

Adaptive Behavior

Walks into its one pre-committed desk-return rule: machine-learning submissions returned without review. Worse, it’s Q4 in experimental psychology — the breadth gradient would never meet a referee who could test it. Acceptance would be burial.

Interface Focus

Stricter than your sheet suspects: invitation-only within theme issues; the system offers only “submit an invited paper.” Scope fit is weak regardless.

Constructivist Foundations

Written rule you meet 0%: in-depth discussion of constructivism is mandatory, and your lineage contains no von Glasersfeld, Maturana, or von Foerster. But note “perceptual coupling” is Maturana and Varela’s own term — your breadth gradient is what von Glasersfeld’s viability predicts. A strong companion piece, not a home.

  • ✓ Subscribe-to-Open 2026 — eight Royal Society journals free for any author (Proc A/B, RSIF, Biology Letters, Interface Focus, Phil Trans A/B, Notes & Records). Right answer in your sheet, wrong reason.
  • ✓ RSOS and Open Biology excluded from S2O — still gold, still charging.
  • ✓ CRKN–Cambridge covers gold, which is what makes EHS actually free. Wiley covers hybrid only (gold gets 10% off); SAGE hybrid (gold 40% off).
  • ✓ Shibasaki et al. 2024 exists and is what it claims to be.
  • ○ Not re-checked: per-journal word limits, editor identities, and the other cited precedents. Strong leads from the agent research — confirm before you act on any single one.

The sequence

EHS now — free, verifiably, with the right referees, and the reference cap turned out not to bind. Then RSOS if £1,400 is payable, since its precedent is near-exact and its review would engage your controls rather than your register; or Cognitive Science if it isn’t. Then BBS — propose the target article once the result is published and citable, using the McGill connection.

Decide one thing first, because it reorders everything above: is your constraint “spend no money” — trivially satisfied almost everywhere, since you can publish subscription-side and green self-archive — or “CC-BY gold OA at no cost”? Those are very different lists.
